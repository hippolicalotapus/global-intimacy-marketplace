## Proposed storefront model (not deployed)
- Customer-facing entry paths: All Products; Men; Women; Couples & Shared Experiences; Gay & Lesbian; Connected Technology; Wellness; ONE HEART Apparel; Creator Gifting; Companion Technology.
- Product category describes WHAT the item is: stroker, ring, external massager, internal massager, anal, lubricant, care, games, lingerie, clothing, robotics, etc.
- Department tags describe OPTIONAL shopping paths; one product may appear in many departments.
- Men's entry path: male-anatomy fit products when confirmed, plus universal and opt-in cross-category items.
- Women's entry path: vulva/vaginal or other anatomically relevant products when confirmed, plus universal and opt-in cross-category items.
- Couples entry path: explicit partner/shared-design products and truly supported two-device / partner compatibility.
- Gay & Lesbian entry path: inclusive discovery across the range with non-stereotyped functional groupings; sexual orientation is never inferred from a product or required from a shopper.
- Universal examples: lubricants (following label/usage directions), care, appropriate massage, many anal devices and apparel by verified size/fit rather than gender. These may appear in several departments.
- Companions/robotics: classify by capabilities/materials/physical type, not by assumed buyer gender.
- Apply tags only after checking supplier specifications; ambiguous items are marked 'fit not confirmed' and remain in All Products. Do not claim that a device is app-controlled or compatible without verifying the exact model.
- Keep product previews unpriced and non-purchasable until authorized media rights, wholesaler costs, fulfillment, payment eligibility and applicable restrictions are documented.
- Add automated QA: direct Men/Women/Couples/Gay-Lesbian route, search, overlapping listings, correct anatomy/compatibility tags, no shopper identity gating, 18+ and preview-only controls. Mobile menu must expose all departments without crowding the bottom nav.

## ONE HEART first revenue/funding track
- ONE HEART has 16 lookbook concepts but no confirmed manufactured stock. Do not describe preview artwork as finished merchandise.
- Choose one hoodie and one art tee for a limited Canada-first launch after print-ready artwork, size charts, quality samples, production supplier, return terms and fulfillment quote are confirmed.
- Build two scenarios: print-on-demand/no upfront inventory; and small-batch production after demonstrated demand.
- Unit economics for each candidate: selling price minus production / print, packaging, outbound shipping, payment fees, promotions, expected returns, and tax treatment. Do not invent costs.
- Build lender-ready: registered legal structure and genuine ownership/disclosures, business plan and narrative, target market, supplier quotes, 12–24 month cash-flow forecast, owner investment/collateral, customer-interest evidence, realistic use-of-funds.
- Keep a clear apparel cost center and account trail. If a lender/funder requires a separate clothing-only entity, evaluate true operational and legal separation, disclosed common ownership, and restricted use of proceeds. Never represent the broader marketplace as non-existent or divert restricted grants/loans to ineligible adult-product activities.

## Verified candidate financing sources (official pages, checked 2026-10-31)
1. Community Futures Yellowhead East — Sangudo in service area; new rural business loans up to CAD $150,000 and planning advice. Requires approaching bank first, a business plan, credit check, personal investment and security; all loans subject to review. https://yellowheadeast.albertacf.com/services/business-loans and https://yellowheadeast.albertacf.com/services/starting-a-business-in-yellowhead-east
2. Canada Small Business Financing Program — eligible Canadian startups may seek financing through banks/credit unions; lender decides. General maximum $1.15M across facilities is not an estimated award or target. Costs and collateral eligibility apply. https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-small-business-financing-program/en/canada-small-business-financing-program/find-loan-your-small-business
3. Futurpreneur — only for eligible 18–39-year-old Canadian entrepreneurs, with additional business-content/activities restrictions and ready-to-market products; do NOT assume the mixed marketplace qualifies. https://futurpreneur.ca/en/eligibility/
4. Alberta Innovates Venture Development Grant — technology innovation with incorporated or eligible registered Alberta business, 25% cash contribution and innovation-readiness evidence. General apparel ecommerce alone is NOT a fit. https://albertainnovates.ca/funding/venture-development-grant/
5. Alberta Manufacturing Productivity Grant — up to $30,000 matching for eligible Alberta manufacturers with 5–750 staff and qualifying equipment/tech; unsuitable for a new print-on-demand-only one-person business. https://www.alberta.ca/index.php/alberta-manufacturing-productivity-grant
6. Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant — training support only; applicants ordinarily need at least one year operating in Alberta, so not a prelaunch financing source. https://www.alberta.ca/canada-alberta-productivity-grant
7. Business Benefits Finder for additional programs: https://innovation.ised-isde.canada.ca/s/?language=en_CA
